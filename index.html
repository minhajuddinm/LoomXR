LoomXR Labs is building a web AR platform for consumer brands. A shopper scans a product in store, answers a few questions about their taste, and sees a packaging design generated for them on the real box, in the phone browser.

What the platform does

Three parts run in one browser session. The shopper installs nothing. The table shows what works in our research prototype today and what we plan for the first release.

PartWhat happensStatus
Recognizes the product The phone camera finds the brand's box and follows it as the shopper moves it. Prototype: printed marker on the box, tracked with AR.js.
Planned: a detection model trained on each brand's product photos, so no marker is needed.
Generates the design The shopper's answers become an image prompt. A design comes back in 5 to 10 seconds. Prototype: FLUX 1.1 [pro] image generation.
Planned: brand rules for colours, logo and protected areas applied to every design.
Shows it on the box The design is mapped onto the box in the camera view. The shopper turns the box and sees each side. Prototype: Three.js texture mapping in a Next.js web app.
Planned: steadier tracking under store lighting.

How a brand would use it

This is the plan for the first release. No brand is using LoomXR yet, and we are looking for the first ones to test it with.

  1. Send product photos

    We train a detection model on the brand's product.

  2. Set the rules

    Colours, logo placement, and areas a design must not cover.

  3. Place a QR code

    On the shelf or on the product. Scanning it opens LoomXR in the browser.

  4. Receive the chosen design

    The shopper picks a design and orders it. The brand gets the file.

LoomXR supplies the software. We do not print or ship packaging. The brand produces each order with its own printer.

The prototype and what we measured

LoomXR started as HYPAR, an honours thesis project at SLIDE Studio, Algoma University. We built a working web AR prototype and tested it with 15 people using a perfume box. Each person generated a design and viewed it on the box through a phone.

What did not work well

The AR view jittered. Tracking dropped when a finger covered the marker, and the design was cropped at steep viewing angles. Lighting on the box also affected detection. Tracking is the first engineering problem we will work on for the product.

M. Minhajuddin, J. Varghese, S. Hu and S. Nam, Designing Hyper-Personalized Retail Product Packaging through Generative AI and Augmented Reality. Accepted at IEEE ISEMV 2026, Larnaca, Cyprus, 22 to 24 October 2026.

How we use Claude

We are three people. Claude is part of how we build and part of what we plan to ship.

Engineering In use

We write, review and refactor the web app with Claude Code. This site was built the same way.

Prompt writing Planned

Claude will turn a shopper's answers and a brand's rules into the prompt sent to the image model, so the shopper never has to write one.

Brand rule checks Planned

Claude will review each generated design against the brand's rules before the shopper sees it, and ask for a new one when it fails.
